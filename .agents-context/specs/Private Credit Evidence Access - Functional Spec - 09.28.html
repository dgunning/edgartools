Private Credit Evidence Access
Functional Spec

Date: September 28, 2026 · Status: user-confirmed scope and end-state · Surface: EdgarTools MCP

Sources used: planning conversation and repository review; AGENTS.md; docs/verification-constitution.md; edgar/ai/mcp/server.py; relevant MCP tools; edgar/bdc investment, non-accrual, reference and dataset modules; edgar/_filings.py; tests/test_bdc_nonaccrual_classifier.py; docs/ai/mcp-tools.md; edgar/ai/skills/funds/skill.yaml. Chosen direction: provide the right tools to retrieve credit evidence from the correct public documents. Analysts or their AI assistants compare periods and interpret lender protections. The MCP does not perform historical matching, score risk or estimate recoveries.

Goal

Enable private credit analysts to identify disclosed signs of borrower deterioration and investigate lender protections using complete, dated and traceable SEC evidence through EdgarTools MCP.

Purpose and private credit value

A private credit firm lends money to earn interest. A substantial loss on one loan can erase earnings from other loans. Its two central questions are whether repayment is becoming less likely and how much money could be lost if repayment fails. This spec improves the evidence available to answer those questions.

Use caseBusiness valueEvidence contribution
Investigate borrower deterioration
“Is repayment becoming less likely?”		Earlier investigation can give a lender more time to seek information, negotiate protections or prepare for restructuring. Easier retrieval also reduces time spent assembling evidence.Named BDC loan holdings, disclosed valuations, interest terms and non-accrual evidence from selected reporting periods.
Research potential losses
“What protects us if repayment fails?”		Understanding collateral, guarantees and payment priority supports decisions about whether to lend, how much to lend and which protections to require.Access to filed agreements, amendments, guarantees and relevant financial notes, with passages that analysts can inspect in context.

For a Moody’s-type customer, the proposed value is reliable evidence that fits credit research workflows and reduces document collection effort. Public disclosures can complement confidential borrower reports by showing what other reporting lenders disclose. This is the business rationale agreed in the planning conversation, not a claim about Moody’s purchasing plans or a quantified savings forecast.

Success criteria

  • An analyst can identify a borrower and retrieve the available loan facts from a selected annual or quarterly filing, with the period and source unambiguous.
  • All extracted holdings and relevant readable evidence are reachable through bounded responses with continuation, rather than inaccessible after a preview limit.
  • Available non-accrual evidence identifies whether it relates to individual investments or a portfolio aggregate and preserves its supporting disclosure.
  • An analyst can discover a relevant filed agreement, locate passages and read surrounding context without leaving MCP for manual document retrieval.
  • Missing data, partial extraction and explicit reported zero values remain distinguishable.
  • Tool descriptions and examples let an agent discover these workflows without reading repository source code.

Non-goals and hard constraints

Non-goals: automatic loan matching across filings or lenders; computed historical changes; automated risk flags or monitoring services; credit ratings; calculated recovery estimates; standardized legal-term extraction; legal conclusions; a complete private-borrower financial database.

Constraints: public SEC evidence delivered through the existing EdgarTools MCP. Preserve the distinction between what a document reports and what an analyst infers. Disclosure and extraction limitations must be visible. This document specifies capabilities and behavior, not architecture or implementation tasks.

Project quality requirements are grounded in AGENTS.md:85–98 and docs/verification-constitution.md:13–55: correctness, useful failure behavior and agent discoverability are part of the feature promise.

Desired end-state

1. Select the evidenceIdentify the reporting entity, filing, period and document.
2. Retrieve through MCPGet available loan facts, footnotes, tables and document passages with sources and coverage information.
3. Analyst or AI interpretsCompare periods and assess repayment concerns or contractual protections outside the MCP’s retrieval responsibility.

Functional requirements

CapabilityRequired observable behavior
F1. Correct filing selectionDiscover and select specific annual or quarterly filings. Holdings and notes must be retrievable for that selected filing, rather than silently replaced with the latest annual report. Return reporting entity, form, accession, reporting period, filing date and source link. Distinguish original filings from amendments where applicable. A failed explicit selection produces a useful explanation rather than unrelated data.
F2. Borrower and loan evidencePreserve the reported borrower name, investment identifier and investment description. Surface available fair value, cost, principal, interest rate, spread and PIK rate with clear units and rate conventions. Preserve available source labels. Maturity or lien information can be retrieved from supporting documents or datasets where present; universal structured extraction is not promised. Do not collapse distinct facilities into one borrower record or imply a globally stable identifier.
F3. Complete access to extracted holdingsAllow borrower filtering and continuation through the complete extracted result. Show returned scope, remaining results and extraction limitations. Distinguish totals for the full extracted portfolio from a filtered subset or response page. “All extracted holdings” must not imply that every holding in the original filing was successfully parsed.
F4. Non-accrual evidenceReturn available individual investment evidence, related footnote text and its source. If only portfolio-level data is available, label it as aggregate evidence. Preserve extraction method and meaningful warnings. Absence from an extracted list does not establish that a loan is performing. Do not convert non-accrual accounting status into a legal default determination.
F5. Notes and narrative accessRetrieve relevant notes, table rows and filing passages from the selected document. Previews explicitly identify omitted content and provide a way to continue. Context around a passage remains accessible, including relevant headings and footnotes. Support research into debt, liquidity and related disclosures without promising an automated interpretation.
F6. Agreement discoveryList filed documents and exhibits with available titles or descriptions, document type, filename or document identifier and source link. Search relevant filings and document text to locate agreements, amendments and guarantees. Distinguish the main filing from an exhibit. Preserve available references to documents filed elsewhere so the caller can pursue the referenced filing; do not claim an automatically reconstructed complete agreement history.
F7. Specific-document readingRead the chosen exhibit itself and search within it. Return matching passages, surrounding context and a locator tied to that document. Support continuation for long readable documents. An exhibit selection must not resolve silently to a parent-filing summary. If content cannot be read, return its identity, source link and the reason it is unavailable.
F8. Provenance and honest gapsReturned evidence must be traceable to its filing and, where relevant, its document, note, table or passage. Distinguish reporting period from publication date. Separate explicit reported zero, not disclosed, not extracted, unsupported content and retrieval failure where the evidence permits that distinction. When the cause is unknown, say so. Preserve the reporting lender versus underlying borrower distinction.
F9. Discoverable workflowsTool descriptions and examples guide agents from a borrower or BDC question to the correct filing and supporting evidence. Continuation, source selection and error recovery are discoverable. Exact tool names and parameter layouts are outside this functional spec.

Representative user outcomes

  • Two-period research: an analyst retrieves a named borrower’s available holdings from two selected BDC filings. Each result retains its own dates, values and source. The MCP makes no claim that records match or that a value change proves deterioration.
  • Distress investigation: an analyst retrieves a disclosed non-accrual investment and its supporting footnote. If only a portfolio aggregate is available, the response does not assign that status to specific borrowers.
  • Recovery research: an analyst finds a filed agreement and amendment, searches for guarantees or collateral and reads the surrounding provisions. The analyst determines their meaning and whether further documents are needed.
  • Long or incomplete evidence: an analyst can continue beyond a holdings page or document excerpt. Unreadable content and extraction gaps remain visible rather than appearing as proof that no protection or risk exists.

Plain-language terms and interpretation limits

BDC: a business development company that reports its investments, providing a public window into some private-company loans. PIK: payment-in-kind interest, added to debt rather than paid in cash; its presence alone does not establish distress. Non-accrual: an accounting treatment under which a lender stops recognizing interest income because collection is uncertain. Collateral: assets supporting a loan. Guarantee: another party’s contractual commitment to support an obligation. Repayment priority: the order in which creditors may be paid, subject to the governing documents and circumstances.

A reported holding is the reporting lender’s position, not the borrower’s total debt. A valuation mark is not a realized recovery. A BDC’s own borrowing documents must not be mistaken for the loan agreements of its portfolio companies. Public evidence may not contain the entire agreement, all amendments or the borrower’s complete finances. These boundaries carry forward the user-confirmed end-state and the planning conversation’s rationale.

Explicit remainder outside scope

No historical comparison engine, entity-resolution service, calculated change feed, risk score, automatic alert, legal-term summary schema or recovery model. No confidential borrower data ingestion. No promise of complete market coverage, automatic reconstruction of all contract amendments or access to unfiled agreements.

Assumptions & open questions

  • No unresolved scope choice: the user confirmed both evidence-access use cases, the two interpretation boundaries and the desired end-state.
  • Coverage remains unmeasured: this review established code paths and a local simulated response, not live success rates across BDCs or document formats. No numeric coverage target is asserted.
  • Document formats: exhaustive OCR or scanned-PDF extraction was not agreed. Unreadable documents must remain discoverable with an explicit limitation; this spec does not promise new OCR capability.
  • Delivery constraints: no deadline, latency target or staffing budget was supplied. The confirmed constraints are public SEC data, the existing MCP surface and evidence retrieval without analytical interpretation.
  • Design details intentionally deferred: tool names, parameter schemas, continuation mechanics and exact citation representation remain design choices, provided the observable outcomes above are met.
