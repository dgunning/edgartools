**A ratio could divide one period's number by another's.** `calculate_ratios()` fetched every period at once and let each operand independently take the first value in its own dictionary — insertion order, not recency — so Netflix's Q3 2024 net margin came back as 0.2767: that quarter's net income over the *prior* year's revenue, a figure belonging to neither quarter against a correct 0.2406. `CurrentPeriodStatement.calculate_ratios()` compounded it by delegating to the unfiltered statement, so even an explicitly narrowed current-period object returned the mixed answer while its own `get_raw_data()` held the right operands. Ratios now take every operand from one period, which defaults to the statement's most recent and can be named explicitly, and a ratio whose operands are not all present for that period is omitted rather than borrowed. Fourteen ratio results change across the fixture corpus, each moving onto the period the statement actually reports — Microsoft FY2015 to a 64.7% gross margin on its filed $93,580M of revenue. The existing `test_ysr8_statement_helpers` expectation of 0.3520 for Apple FY2010 was itself the defect — that is FY2008, the oldest comparative — and now asserts the filed 0.3938.
